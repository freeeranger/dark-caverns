## Design specificity verdict

The visual language is clearly Dark Caverns. Minecraft inventory slots, hard bevels, pixel typography, luminite cyan, material colors, crafting diagrams, and actual mod textures would not transfer cleanly to an unrelated product. Rubik handles long text while the pixel font stays focused on headings and controls.

The page choreography is less specific. Overview follows a standard hero, trailer, and feature-card sequence. Guides and Wiki use a familiar sidebar and article split. Download is a centered pair of outbound buttons. Remove the textures and font, and much of the structure becomes a generic documentation template. The cavern world should affect scale, pacing, imagery, and movement, not only the UI chrome.

The deterministic scan returned zero findings for `src`. It reported valid JSON, no parser warning, and no false positives. The root `404.html` was outside that scan target. The required browser control was unavailable, so no live overlay exists. The fallback evidence was source inspection plus successful HTTP checks for every route.

## Overall impression

This is a solid mod site with a real identity and unusually useful documentation. Its strongest pages help existing players find precise answers. Its weakest moments are the places where a visitor must transition: deciding to download, choosing content on a phone, recovering from a bad URL, or understanding an empty filter. The biggest opportunity is to connect the site's strong Minecraft UI system to a more atmospheric journey through the dimension.

## What's working

1. The visual system is specific and disciplined. Inventory slots, bevel direction, pixel rendering, luminite states, and real item textures feel native to the mod.
2. The information architecture matches player intent. Gateway access, exploration, progression, recipes, and downloads are organized around tasks players perform.
3. The accessibility base is stronger than the aesthetic suggests. The site uses native controls, visible focus styling, `aria-current`, labelled search and icon actions, headings, table headers, and useful image alternatives.

## Cognitive load

Overall load is moderate, with three failures in the eight-point checklist.

- Chunking fails in the Wiki's six category choices and the Guide selector's five peer chapters.
- Minimal choices fails in the Wiki, where six categories sit above roughly eight visible entries.
- Working memory fails on mobile because the selector remains above the changed article without scrolling, focus movement, or an announcement.

Grouping, hierarchy, single focus, one-at-a-time reading, and progressive disclosure work well. Decision points above four visible options are the six Wiki categories, the Wiki entry list, and the five Guide chapters.

## Emotional journey

The opening establishes Minecraft and the mod quickly. The trailer is the emotional high point. After that, repeated boxes turn discovery into an inventory of features, and the Overview ends without a final reveal or renewed download action.

The Download page arrives at a high-trust moment but gives little reassurance beyond the game version, loader, and GeckoLib requirement. It does not explain whether Modrinth and CurseForge are equivalent choices or give a short installation path.

Guides and Wiki support competence on desktop. On mobile, tapping a selector without an immediate transition creates doubt about whether anything happened. The 404 line is a good thematic recovery beat, but faulty nested links can turn it into another failure.

## Priority issues

### 1. [P1] Mobile selection does not transition to the chosen content

Why it matters: Below the desktop breakpoint, the Guide and Wiki selectors stack above the article. Selecting an item changes content far below the tap without scrolling, focusing, collapsing the selector, or announcing the new title.

Fix: Use a mobile drill-in flow with a visible return action. A smaller change would collapse the selector after selection, focus and scroll to the article heading, and announce the new title through a polite live region.

Suggested command: `$impeccable adapt`

### 2. [P1] The 404 recovery links can lead to another 404

Why it matters: The links use relative paths. From a nested missing URL, Guides and Wiki can resolve under that missing directory. The recovery page becomes a trap when trust is already low.

Fix: Use site-root-aware paths, load the actual pixel font, replace the styled title `div` with an `h1`, add visible focus treatment, and restore enough global navigation to orient the user.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

### 3. [P2] Overview and Download explain the mod without fully selling or reassuring

Why it matters: Most world imagery appears at inventory-icon scale. The trailer carries nearly all the atmosphere. Download asks visitors to leave for one of two providers without explaining the choice, install order, or current-release confidence.

Fix: Give the first viewport one strong in-game image or cavern composition, use larger world imagery deeper on the page, add a final action after the feature catalogue, and turn compatibility details into a compact install block.

Suggested commands: `$impeccable bolder`, then `$impeccable clarify`

### 4. [P2] Wiki filters can contradict the visible article and have no empty state

Why it matters: The selected article is independent of filtered results. A filter can remove the selected row while its article remains visible. A zero-result search leaves an empty list with only a count.

Fix: Either label the article as outside the current filter or select the first result when filters change. Add a zero-result message with actions to clear the search and show all categories.

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

### 5. [P2] Touch size and composite control semantics trail the visual craft

Why it matters: Several navigation, category, clear-search, Guide, and footer controls are smaller than 44 pixels. The declared tablist and listbox patterns lack arrow-key behavior and relationships. Recipe tooltips are not reliably available to keyboard and touch users. Footer text contrast is about 3.95:1 at a small size.

Fix: Increase mobile targets, retain a visible search focus state, implement complete composite keyboard behavior or use simpler semantics, expose recipe names without hover, and raise footer contrast. Registry ID selection and copying were fixed during this critique.

Suggested command: `$impeccable audit`

## Persona red flags

### Jordan, first-time visitor

Jordan can understand the premise and find the 1.21.1 download quickly. NeoForge and GeckoLib appear at the point of commitment without enough context. The two download providers have no explanation, and an empty Wiki search offers no next step.

### Sam, keyboard and assistive-technology user

Sam benefits from semantic headings, native controls, labels, and the shared focus ring. The search removes its local outline, ARIA composite roles lack their expected keyboard behavior, recipe relationships are mainly spatial, small controls increase motor demands, and footer contrast misses the normal-text target.

### Casey, distracted mobile visitor

Casey sees a two-row sticky header and dense category controls. Selecting a Guide or Wiki item does not bring the result into view. Remote texture requests have no loading or failure state on a slow connection. Download actions stack well, but the dependency and installation implications are easy to miss.

## Minor observations

- All normal routes use the same document title, which weakens browser history and assistive navigation.
- External-link icons are hidden from assistive technology, and the links do not announce that they open a new tab.
- `JetBrains Mono` is declared but not loaded, so registry IDs use a generic monospace fallback.
- The Overview ends after its feature groups without a concluding action.
- The Credits page is consistent but sparse.
- Remote textures have stable containers but no lazy loading or broken-image fallback.
- The footer's four-link row and the unwrapped Smithing Table may become tight under high zoom.

## Questions to consider

- Should the first viewport lead with claustrophobia, discovery, danger, or luminous wonder?
- Should the mobile Wiki become a true drill-in encyclopedia instead of a stacked desktop layout?
- Which reassurance matters most before download: loader compatibility, dependency order, install steps, or release freshness?
- Could the Overview end with a deliberate return to the gateway and download decision?
